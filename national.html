📝 Short Range (Days 1-2)

Valid 12Z Sat Oct 03 2026 - 12Z Mon Oct 05 2026 · issued Fri 8 PM ET · Kong
WPC forecaster narrative for the next two days: surface systems, QPF reasoning, severe potential.

...Excessive rainfall threat across the Southeast on Sunday while continuing across the central Gulf Coast and southern Texas while this weekend...

...A long duration October heatwave emerging across central and southern California before spreading into the Great Basin...

Over the next couple of days, the excessive rainfall threat will remain focused along the Gulf Coast to the Southeast as an anomalous October heatwave builds across the western U.S. The lingering wet weather pattern results from a cool and dry air mass across the northern tier of the country interacting with a tropical wave over the Gulf. The Weather Prediction Center maintains a slight risk (at least 15% chance) of excessive rainfall for today near the central Gulf Coast to the Florida Panhandle in addition to the lower Texas Gulf Coast. By Sunday, the excessive rainfall threat is forecast to lift northeastward across the interior Southeast and into the Carolinas with the passage of a weak upper trough. A few additional inches of heavy rain can be expected along the central Gulf Coast, with a maximum of 7 inches possible near the immediate coast of Alabama. The heavy rainfall threat will gradually decrease from Sunday night to Monday morning for the Southeast and along the Gulf Coast as the trough nudges the front farther south.

Meanwhile, a low pressure system will sweep across the northern tier states bringing scattered to isolated showers and thunderstorms to the Upper Midwest/Great Lakes from today into Sunday morning. By early Monday morning, a stronger cold front trailing south from the intensifying low pressure system in Quebec could bring heavy showers and embedded thunderstorms into interior New England. Across the Mid-Atlantic, showers behind a cold front early this morning will move off the coast as the cool air mass from the Ohio Valley to the Northeast takes over.

As the wet pattern persists across the South, an anomalous ridge of high pressure will help build a late season October heatwave across the western U.S. through the weekend and into next week. Temperatures will run 15 to 20 degrees above normal, bringing highs well into the 90s toward the coast and adjacent urban areas, with some areas in the Central Valley reaching 100. Overnight lows in the mid to upper 70s in the Los Angeles and San Diego urban areas will provide little relief through the heat wave. Extreme heat warnings are in effect for much of coastal southern California, while heat advisories are in effect for central California. The heat will then expand farther northeast over the next couple of days, with high temperatures in the 80s and near 90 degrees emerging across the Great Basin. Elsewhere, clear conditions accompanying a high pressure system have prompted frost advisories and freeze warnings early this morning for portions of northern Wisconsin and Michigan.

A tropical wave moving across the Gulf will funnel record to near record amounts of atmospheric moisture into much of the Gulf Coast on Saturday. A 15-25 kt southerly flow into the coast at 850 mb will support numerous showers and thunderstorms associated with the wave moving into the central Gulf Coast both today and tonight. PWATs will be at or above 2.5 inches for many areas. Extensive cloud cover and the October sun should limit instability considerably with any reasonable distance from the coast. THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF THE SOUTHEAST AND LOWER TEXAS COAST

Day 3

...Southeast...

Very few changes were needed with this update across the Southeast. While the trends in the guidance continue to suggest a southward trend, given the heavier rains from Sunday, the ERO remains generous on the northern side of the rainfall footprint, to account for potential ongoing flooding continuing into Monday. The heaviest rains on Monday will focus in far southeastern Georgia and from Jacksonville north and west in Florida. As on previous days, instability will be at a premium due to extensive cloud cover from the extremely high moisture amounts in the atmosphere. Much of the Florida Peninsula will have PWATs at or above 2.25 inches, supporting highly efficient warm rain processes. This will be countered by the lack of instability, which will limit the lift any storms will have to work with. By Monday, a somewhat faster southward movement of the cold front lifting all this moisture should significantly limit overlap in the forecast rainfall footprints from Sunday to Monday. Given how difficult most of southeast Georgia can be to flood due to flat, sandy terrain and numerous swamps, a high-end Marginal remains in place.

A continued southward trend in the front could allow for trimming on the northern end of the Marginal with future updates.

...Lower Texas Coast...

An overall weakening of both the tropical wave over the Gulf and the subsequent easterly/onshore flow should limit additional rainfall along the immediate coast on Monday. Thus, despite several days of prior rains, the inherited Marginal was left in place with few changes.

Wegman

Day 4 and Day 5

Day 4 (12Z Tue-12Z Wed) -- Little change made to yesterday's Day 5 Marginal Risk area. The Marginal area slips a little farther south into North-Central FL and far southeast GA Tue-Tue night, where the surface front more than likely will begin to stall and become quasi- stationary. The axis of highest PWs (2-2.5") and mid-level shortwave energy will be draped along and south of the front, as will some (albeit weak) deep-layer instability. Deep west/west- southwesterly flow will enhanced the risk for training cells coming off the eastern Gulf, as all the global ensembles (GEFS/ECENS/CMC) have at least a few members noting rainfall totals of 3-5+" within the 24hr period. However it should be noted the QPF (deterministic and probabilistic) trends in the guidance has decreased over the past couple of model cycles, with now the highest 3-5+ inch QPF probabilities over the waters of eastern Gulf and off the Atlantic Coast per the GEFS/ECENS/CMC (where deep-layer instability is more conducive).

For now, we did not include another Marginal Risk in the Day 5 ERO (12Z Wed-12Z Thu) across Central FL. Yes the front will remain hung up in the vicinity, however the heavy rainfall signal is more diffuse with the 0-6km Bulk Shear values over 20kts along and north of the front, implying more anafrontal rainfall where the deep- layer instability (elevated) is marginal at best. Areal spread in the QPF among the global models is also rather high, again with the heavier amounts over the water (Gulf and Atlantic) where the instability is decidedly more favorable. Meanwhile, of late the AI guidance (both ECMWF AIFS and AIGFS QPF) have verified better of late, largely due to the AI models having a better handle on the frontal progression. By day 5, both the AIGFS and EC AIFS show the QPF axis a bit farther south (Central-South FL) compared to the rest of the guidance, and not nearly as heavy with the amounts compared to the latest NBM. Given the high FFG over this area, and the concern that the rates will not be sufficiently high given the lack of deep-layer instability, we opted to refrain from hoisting a Marginal Risk area.
