...There is a Slight Risk (level 2/5) of severe thunderstorms over parts of the Southwest/Southern Rockies on Saturday...

An upper-level low over Southern California and the Southwest by Saturday and will be quasi-stationary through Sunday morning. Moisture from the Gulf of California will stream into the Southwest/Southern High Plains, producing showers and severe thunderstorms. Therefore, the SPC has issued a Slight Risk (level 2/5) of severe thunderstorms over parts of the Southern High Plains and the Southwest through early Sunday morning. The hazards associated with these thunderstorms are frequent lightning, severe thunderstorm wind gusts, hail, and a few tornadoes.

In addition, the showers and thunderstorms will produce heavy rain. Therefore, the WPC has issued a Slight Risk (level 2/4) of excessive rainfall with these thunderstorms over parts of the Central/Southern High Plains and Central/Southern Rockies on Saturday. The associated heavy rain will create mainly localized areas of flash flooding, with urban areas, roads, small streams, narrow canyons/gullies, and burn scars the most vulnerable.

Overnight Saturday, a front over the Eastern Pacific will move onshore over the Pacific Northwest, producing rain over parts of the Pacific Northwest late Saturday night and early Sunday. The rain will continue over the region through Monday.

Moreover, on Sunday, showers and thunderstorms with heavy rain will continue to develop over parts of the Central/Southern High Plains. Therefore, the WPC has issued a Slight Risk (level 2/4) of excessive rainfall with these thunderstorms over parts of the Central/Southern High Plains. The associated heavy rain will create mainly localized areas of flash flooding, with urban areas, roads, small streams, narrow canyons/gullies, and burn scars the most vulnerable.

Also on Sunday, easterly flow off the Atlantic will produce showers and thunderstorms with heavy rain over the eastern coast of Florida. Therefore, the WPC has issued a Slight Risk (level 2/4) of excessive rainfall with these thunderstorms over parts of the eastern coast of Florida. The associated heavy rain will create mainly localized areas of flash flooding, with urban areas, roads, small streams, and low-lying areas being the most vulnerable.

🌧️ Excessive Rainfall - Days 1-2

Valid 12Z Sat Oct 24 2026 - 12Z Sun Oct 25 2026 · issued Fri 7:29 PM ET
THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF WEST TEXAS, NEW MEXICO, AND COLORADO
THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF THE CENTRAL AND SOUTHERN HIGH PLAINS AND SOUTHEAST FLORIDA
THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF THE CENTRAL AND SOUTHERN HIGH PLAINS AND SOUTHEAST FLORIDA

Day 1

...West Texas to the Southern/Central High Plains...

An upper level low will split off from the main upper trough over the southwestern U.S. early this morning. This feature will create favorable conditions downstream for scattered to widespread convection to develop from West Texas and New Mexico into the southern and central High Plains, and convection will likely be enhanced in coverage and intensity by an upper level shortwave lifting north across the region this afternoon and evening. Additionally, a strengthening south-southeasterly jet ahead of the system will increase moisture transport into the region, and instability will combine with this anomalous moisture to create a heavy rainfall threat.

A Slight Risk of excessive rainfall was maintained for much of eastern New Mexico and southeastern Colorado and portions of West Texas and western Kansas. This area is expected to see the highest rainfall totals ranging from 1.5-3 inches, with higher amounts expected in portions of northeastern New Mexico and southeastern Colorado where the HREF and REFS show 50-75% chance of exceeding 3 inches in 24 hours. Rainfall rates in this region may reach 1-1.5 inches per hour, rivaling flash flood guidance, and could lead to scattered instances of flash flooding. Thankfully, the heaviest rainfall today should be displaced to the east of where the heaviest rain fell yesterday, but portions of north-central New Mexico and south-central Colorado have been included in the Slight Risk area to account for an elevated flash flood threat where locally heavy rain may fall over already saturated soils.

The Slight Risk area also extends south into southeastern New Mexico and West Texas where the environment will be favorable for strong, organized convection later today. This area will see the highest CAPE and wind shear values this afternoon, and the vertically veering wind profile will be conducive for supercell development. Any supercells that develop will be capable of producing locally heavy rainfall that could cause flash flooding. These cells should be fairly quick-moving, which should help to somewhat limit the flash flood risk. The surrounding Marginal Risk area was expanded in West Texas to account for the potential eastward progression of convection this evening and for an upward trend in hi-res model QPF in this area.

...Four Corners region...

A Marginal Risk of excessive rainfall extends back into the Four Corners region and includes portions of eastern and southern Utah and northern Arizona where convection in the vicinity of the upper low may result in isolated instances of flash flooding. Convection in this area should be fairly slow moving, and although snow levels will be dropping, they should remain high enough to allow for heavy rainfall. Models show some instability developing across Utah and Arizona during the afternoon hours, and there should be enough moisture to support widely scattered convection. Given the preceding days of heavy rainfall leading up to Saturday, the region may be more sensitive to additional heavy rainfall.

THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF THE CENTRAL AND SOUTHERN HIGH PLAINS AND SOUTHEAST FLORIDA

Day 3

...Central and Southern High Plains...

The upper level cutoff low will progress further, tracking across the north-central U.S. on Monday with a trailing frontal zone extending back across the central and southern High Plains. Scattered convection is expected to develop ahead of the system and will be able to tap into moisture flowing in from the Gulf and instability in the vicinity of the front. A shortwave is expected to move across the region on Monday and a strengthening jet along the frontal boundary will place the southern High Plains in the right entrance region, both of which should provide additional support for convective development. Compared to previous days, model QPF totals are modest, but the ingredients will be in place for at least a localized heavy rainfall threat. Therefore, the inherited Marginal Risk area was continued for the central and southern High Plains.

...Southeast Florida...

Northeasterly onshore flow will continue across Southeast Florida, but the low pressure system will gradually weaken, resulting in precipitation tapering off on Monday night. Given heavy rainfall expected in Southeast Florida on Sunday, a Marginal Risk was maintained to account for isolated flash flood potential with locally heavy rain falling over saturated soils.
