The long advertised cold front extending across the Ohio Valley continues an eastward track, and will spread showers and thunderstorms into the Eastern Seaboard this morning. THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL ACROSS SOUTH-CENTRAL AND SOUTH TEXAS

Day 3

Generally maintained the inherited Marginal Risk area from yesterday's Day 4 ERO, though shifting a bit farther east based on the latest guidance trends.

Model guidance is beginning to converge on a synoptic evolution that would favor areas of heavy rain over southern TX this weekend. A mid-upper low is forecast to dive southeast out of AZ and into northern MX late Friday/Fri night, then gradually progress eastward towards West TX by Sunday. This feature will provide ample ascent for rainfall, with a strong upper level jet providing additional lift. Anomalous moisture will advect northward ahead of this system, and while PWs will likely not be as high as recent events, it is still forecast to exceed the climatological 90th percentile. In the low levels we should have east to northeasterly flow, which will aid in isentropic ascent over the region, but should also act as a stabilizing factor. Thus the degree of instability, which will impact rainfall rates and the flash flood potential, remains a question.

In terms of the latest guidance, the ECMWF AIFS continues to be the most persistent model showing the heavy rainfall threat, and is gaining more support from the operational ECMWF, GFS, and CMC. If/where sufficient destabilization is able to occur to drive higher rainfall rates, a future Slight risk upgrade may be necessary. Best chances for this at the moment appear to be along the south TX coast where instability advecting in off the water and coastal convergence could focus higher rainfall magnitudes.

B Hurley/Chenard
