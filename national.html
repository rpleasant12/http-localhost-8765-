The strong surface low over southeastern Canada will continue to move northeastward, as the strong cold front moves offshore, allowing a colder and drier airmass to spread southeastward behind the boundary. Across the Great Lakes and higher elevations of the Northeast, lingering cyclonic flow and colder air aloft will support mixed precipiation through early Saturday morning. Furthermore, the slow-moving trailing cold front will continue to move southeastward across Florida through early Sunday, bringing showers and thunderstorms across the area. As the boundary interacts with unstable moist air, rainfall rates may increase, leading to excessive rainfall/localized flooding across portions of southeastern Florida coast through Sunday.

Meanwhile, the development of a non-tropical coastal low near the Carolina coast will bring unsettled weather along portions of the Mid-Atlantic coastline through the weekend into Monday. Strengthening onshore flow north of the low will bring periods of heavy rain, gusty winds, and hazardous marine conditions to portions of the coastal Carolinas and Mid-Atlantic. The greatest coastal impacts and heavy rainfall will depend on the storms track, intensity, and proximity to the shoreline. Persistent onshore winds and elevated wave heights could contribute to coastal flooding, beach erosion, and hazardous surf.

Across the West, the most notable concerns will remain concentrated over southern and interior California, where strong offshore flow associated with an enhanced surface pressure gradient will favor Santa Ana wind conditions. The combination of low humidity and locally strong wind gusts will support elevated to critical fire weather conditions through Sunday, particularly across wind-prone passes, foothills, and adjacent inland valleys. Elsewhere across western U.S., a frontal disturbance approaching the Pacific Northwest may produce periods of light showers through early Saturday, especially across coastal areas and the northern Cascades. Additional isolated showers may develop across portions of the Southwest in association with lingering shortwave disturbances through Monday.

A strong surface high pressure will build southeastward behind the cold front, bringing significant cooler temperatures across central, southern, and eastern U.S. Highs will drop 15-25 degrees below normal across the Southern Plains into the Mid-Atlantic/Ohio Valley and Northeast. Widespread Freeze Warnings and Frost Advisories are in effect for portions of the Plains into the Midwest, Great Lakes, Ohio Valley/Mid-Atlantic through Sunday.

Day 4 and Day 5

...Southwest... For Tuesday/Tuesday night, the Southwest is on the eastern periphery of a deep layer cyclone/"outside slider" moving west of CA. The atmosphere still doesn't appear to be sufficiently saturated with 0.75-1" precipitable water values in an environment with mild 1000-500 hPa thickness values. MU CAPE also appears skimpy, with only small pockets of 250 J/kg forecast by the 00z GFS. Continue not to carry a Marginal Risk since guidance is maintaining serve over its past day of runs.

The situation changes markedly on Wednesday/Wednesday night, as the deep layer cyclone approaches SoCal and scoops up moisture from a dying Trudy and flings it into the Southwest. Near the cold low itself, MU CAPE of 250-500 J/kg and precipitable water values of 1-1.3" with up to 30 kts of onshore flow at 850 hPa is forecast. There's enough of a heavy rain signal for a Marginal Risk for the Transverse and Peninsular Ranges, which is there to help cover burn scar concerns. In and near the Four Corners, MU CAPE is forecast to rise above 1000 J/kg, precipitable water values of 0.75-1.25" are anticipated, and a few pieces of deterministic guidance are definitely wet enough for a Marginal Risk with local 3-5" totals. Hourly rain amounts to 2" would be possible across portions of AZ, NM, and the Four Corners. It is possible that this area could carry a greater risk as we move closer to this weather setup.

...Eastern NC/Southeast VA... The guidance advertises a Nor'easter whose center is offshore North Carolina, with an increasing signal for interior convection as -18C air at 500 hPa moves over 28-29C sea surface temperatures during the short range period and perhaps a convective comma head/TROWAL which feeds off additional instability from the western subtropical North Atlantic and tries to brush the NC coast. The guidance has almost completely trended away from heavy rainfall with the system Tuesday/Tuesday night, so removed the Marginal Risk. Wednesday/Wednesday night is a question mark, as the 00z Canadian guidance brings heavy rain back into eastern NC at that time range. As most other guidance has left heavy rainfall offshore, left any Marginal Risks out of the EROs for this system for days 4-5. There is a non-zero chance of excessive rainfall/ flash flooding should a solution like the 00z Canadian come to pass or the guidance tries to slosh/waver back westward in later runs. Stay tuned.
