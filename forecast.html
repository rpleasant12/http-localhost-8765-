DISCUSSION
Issued at 123 AM EDT Mon Sep 21 2026

A weak shortwave will move into the Ohio valley during the day 
Monday and then push east of the forecast area by Tuesday morning, 
all of which is ahead of a closed upper low shifting east through 
the northern plains and into the Upper Midwest. At the surface, this 
wave will push a weak cold front towards the Tennessee valley during 
the day, lingering just to our west and northwest Monday night 
through Tuesday. As such, expect this to be a focus for increased 
convective coverage both Monday and Tuesday. Forecast soundings show 
enough instability to be concerned about a few strong to severe 
storms on Monday capable of damaging winds. The overall threat isn't 
high, but the combination of decent instability along with high 
PWATs means that a few damaging wind gusts similar to what was seen 
a day or two ago could occur. Additionally, with slow storm motions, 
some isolated flash flooding could occur. Tuesday will be similar in 
coverage as the Ohio valley upper low begins to shift southeast, but 
instability will be notably less so outside of the far southern 
parts of the area, think that any severe threat is low.

Regardless of the expected storms for Monday and Tuesday, the heat 
will continue. Highs in the 80s and 90s are expected.

On Wednesday the pattern change gets underway and we begin to see a 
break in the relentless heat. By Wednesday a rex block pattern takes 
shape as an upper ridge builds over the Canadian side of the Great 
Lakes region, and the aforementioned Ohio valley upper low deepens 
over the Ohio valley and central Appalachian region. With the upper 
low to our north and NW flow aloft, this will push the cold front 
through the southern Appalachians and allow the cooldown to begin. 
By Thursday through the weekend we should see highs topping out only 
in the 70's for every except southeast TN where they may squeak 
across the 80 degree mark. This pattern will also be fairly dry with 
a more Canadian airmass moving in.
