🌪️ Storm Chaser's Field Guide — reading the composites & SPC colors together

This guide teaches one skill: walking onto the Models page, reading the severe-parameter walls — SCP, STP, EHI (and SHIP for hail) — and knowing instantly what the colors are telling you, because every wall, the Severe page outlooks and the Mesoanalysis composites all speak the same six-color SPC language.

🎨 The SPC color language — one ladder, everywhere

SPC's categorical outlook colors double as the fill ramp on the severe walls. Memorize the ladder once and every map on this site reads the same way — a yellow blob means slight-risk-grade environment whether it's an outlook polygon, an SCP wall, or a mesoanalysis composite:

TSTM — unorganized — storms, if any, stay ordinary
MRGL — marginal — isolated severe possible
SLGT — slight — scattered severe storms
ENH — enhanced — numerous severe storms
MDT — moderate — widespread severe likely
HIGH — rare — long-track strong tornado / MCS outbreak

On the walls the ramp is continuous (values blend between bands); the hex values above are the exact anchors shared with the Severe page and the mesoanalysis composites. A wall that is entirely green is simply saying "no organized-severe environment yet" — that is information, not a bug.

🌪️ SCP — Supercell Composite: can a rotating storm organize here?

Formulamin(MUCAPE/1000, 1.5) × min(ESRH/50, 1.5) × min(EBWD/20, 1.5)

SCP multiplies the three supercell ingredients — instability, storm-relative helicity (spin), and deep-layer shear — so all three must be present for the value to climb. A wall goes yellow-orange only where fuel and spin overlap.

🎯 STP — Significant Tornado Parameter: which end is tornadic?

FormulaSCP's terms at tornado weights × LCL term — low cloud bases raise it

STP is SCP with tornado tuning: it weighs helicity and shear harder and adds a cloud-base term — low LCLs (humid surface air, small T/Td spread) are the tornadic ingredient. Where SCP says "supercells", STP says which part of that area supports tornadoes.

⚡ EHI — Energy Helicity Index: the early spin-and-fuel check

FormulaMUCAPE × ESRH / 160,000

EHI is deliberately simple — just instability × spin. It ignores shear direction and cloud bases, which makes it fast and honest: when EHI climbs toward 1 while the shearing is still organizing, supercells are becoming possible even before SCP agrees. It is often the first of the three to light up as a warm front or dryline sets up.

🧊 SHIP — Significant Hail Parameter: how big might the stones get?

Formulamin(MUCAPE/1000, 1.5) × min(LR75/5.6, 1.5) × min(mid-RH/50, 1.5) × min(EBWD/50, 1.5)

SHIP stacks the four hail ingredients — instability, steep 700–500 mb lapse rates (the growth-zone chill), mid-level moisture, and deep-layer shear (which keeps stones recycling through the growth zone instead of falling out). Like the other composites, each term caps at 1.5 so one extreme can't fake the signal. SHIP says nothing about tornadoes — a SHIP 4 day can carry zero torsional spin.

📸 Hail structure — and when to trade the tornado target for the hail core

Reading hail structure: on radar, big hail announces itself — reflectivity cores above 60 dBZ aloft, a three-body scatter spike (the flaring 'hail spike' downstream of the core), and an unusually deep, tight core pedestal. Visually: greenish-white glow inside the vault, mamma under the anvil, and stones that look like splattered rubber. High-based storms (elevated updraft bases) drop bigger stones at the ground — more fall distance to accelerate and less warm air to melt them.

When to trade the tornado target: tornado priority holds whenever STP ≥ 1 shows anywhere in range or a tornado watch is up — rotation beats photography, every time. Trade to the hail core when the walls split: SHIP ≥ 2 with STP < 0.5 and a fat cap (huge fuel, no spin), high-based storms, or the SCP axis keeps dying while LR75 stays steep. The tell is in the pair, not one map — the SHIP wall next to the STP wall at the same hour makes the call obvious.

Hail intercept is a different geometrynever wait under the core — position on the storm's inflank, shoot wide, and keep the car pointed out with glass away from the wind. Hail kills chasers' windshields far more often than tornadoes kill chasers.
The honest hybridwhen SHIP and STP overlap in space, ride the storm's rear flank where you can frame the structure and stay clear of both cores — the overlap corridor is where the day pays twice.

Hail photography prizes the same discipline as tornado work: a planned exit, distance, and letting the storm come to your camera. The field card's safety rules all still apply.

🧭 The chase-day drill — one workflow, five maps

1. Frame the day. Open the Severe page — the SPC outlook colors set expectations. ENH+ anywhere in your range means today is a driving day.

2. Check the ingredients. On the collage, load MUCAPE and 0–6 km shear at the same hour: fuel ≥ ~2000 J/kg under ≥ ~30 kt shear is the raw supercell pairing.

3. Find the organization zone. Switch to SCP — the yellow-orange axis is your staging area. Play the wall loop to see when it matures.

4. Find the tornado end. Switch to STP — where it crosses 1 inside the SCP axis is your tornado corridor. Advance the hours: corridors migrate with the low-level jet.

5. Verify live. Watch the radar and the mesoanalysis as storms fire — the meso composites show what the storm is actually ingesting. The Models page town-ranking card tells you which communities sit in the parameter air.

When the models disagreedifferent models painting SCP differently = timing/setup uncertainty. The wall's outlier badge flags the odd one out; lean toward the majority and hold a bigger safety margin.

⚠️ Safety — the part that actually matters

The forecast gets you close. The radar, an escape route, and discipline keep you alive. Never core-punch a hook or hail core — approach from the south/southeast, keep an escape route planned, and know the road network before convection fires.
Chase with an out.Always know where you would drive if the storm turns. Night chasing halves your options — treat nocturnal STP axes with extra respect.
Warnings win.Model walls are guidance, not guarantees — when the NWS issues a warning for your location, act on it first and argue with the models later.
