Severe potential...Watch unlikelyparts of western Kansas and northwestern Oklahoma · valid Thu 2:36 PM ET - Thu 5 PM ET · watch probability 20%
Increasing thunderstorm development may slowly strengthen and become better organized while spreading northeastward through 4-6 PM CDT. The associated risk for severe wind and hail appears mostly marginal in nature and it still appears unlikely that a severe weather watch will be needed. However, trends will continue to be monitored.
A couple of weak/low-amplitude short wave perturbations are progressing through modest southwesterly flow across the Texas South Plains through central Great Plains early this afternoon. This is occurring on the western periphery of fairly prominent mid-level ridging centered (in the 700-500 mb layer) across the southeastern Great Plains. In the wake of the lead perturbation, and in advance of the trailing one, latest objective analysis indicates that a corridor of stronger boundary-layer destabilization is underway near the western Oklahoma/Texas border vicinity through southwestern Kansas. This is occurring in response to low-level moistening and insolation along the weaker southwestern periphery of expansive cool surface ridging (centered over Quebec), and forecast to slowly shift north-northeastward through 21-23Z. As it does, Rapid Refresh output suggests that mixed-layer CAPE may continue increase through the 1000-2000 J/kg range, and the environment may gradually become more conducive to organizing thunderstorm development in the presence of modest shear. Beneath mid-level flow on the order of 30 kt, this could perhaps include evolving supercell structures and an upscale growing cluster. However, rather modest lower/mid-tropospheric lapse rates and temperature/dew point spreads seem likely to result in mostly marginal severe wind and hail potential.
